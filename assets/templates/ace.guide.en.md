# How to use ACE

> A three-box system by Nick Milo (Linking Your Thinking). You split things by what you want to do with them: understand them (Atlas), follow them by date (Calendar) or act on them (Efforts).

## What goes in each folder

| Folder | What goes in | What doesn't |
|---|---|---|
| **Atlas** | What you've learned and collected | Records where the date matters |
| **Atlas/Cards** | Notes that sum up ideas and concepts in your own words | Other people's original text |
| **Atlas/Sources** | Books, articles, videos and your summaries of them | Your to-dos |
| **Calendar** | Dated records: meeting notes, journals, notes from the day | Knowledge that doesn't depend on a date |
| **Efforts** | What you're working on, one folder per effort | Things you're done with and won't look at again |

## Example tree

```
Atlas/
├─ Cards/
│  └─ Building good habits.md
└─ Sources/
   └─ Atomic Habits summary.md
Calendar/
└─ 2026-11-24_Weekly meeting.md
Efforts/
└─ Start a blog/
```

## Naming rules

| What | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Calendar records | date_content | `2026-11-24_Weekly meeting.md` |
| Effort folders | Name of the work | `Start a blog` |

## Keeping it tidy

- Keep the box names in English. They match other people's explanations and examples, so they're easy to follow.
- Start with just Cards and Sources inside Atlas, and add boxes only when you need them.
- When an effort is finished, move its whole folder to Atlas or to an archive folder.

## When another style fits better

- You have more work documents than notes → **PARA**
- You want to manage the flow of to-dos → **GTD (Getting Things Done)**

Source: Nick Milo, "The ACE Folder Framework", https://blog.linkingyourthinking.com/notes/ace-folder-framework
