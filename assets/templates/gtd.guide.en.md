# How to use GTD

> The method from David Allen's book *Getting Things Done* (2001, revised 2015). Don't keep things in your head: collect everything in one place first, then sort each item by asking "is there something to do?"

## What goes in each folder

| Folder | What goes in | What doesn't |
|---|---|---|
| **0_In** | New files, downloads, scanned paper. Everything lands here first | Things to keep for long (empty it often) |
| **1_Next Actions** | Material for things you can do right away. E.g. a form to fill in | Work that needs several steps |
| **2_Projects** | Anything that takes more than one step, one folder each. E.g. Finish the move | Finished work (delete it or move it to reference) |
| **3_Waiting For** | Material for things you handed off or are waiting to hear back on. E.g. a quote request you sent | Things you have to do yourself |
| **4_Someday-Maybe** | Not now, but maybe later. E.g. things to learn, trip ideas | Things for this week |
| **5_Reference** | No action needed, but worth keeping. One folder per topic, in alphabetical order | Material with actions left |

Things with a fixed date go on your calendar app, not in a folder.

## Example tree

```
0_In/
1_Next Actions/
2_Projects/
├─ Renew passport/
└─ Finish the move/
3_Waiting For/
4_Someday-Maybe/
5_Reference/
├─ Car/
└─ Insurance/
```

## Naming rules

| What | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Project folders | The outcome you want | `Renew passport` |
| Files | date_content | `2026-11-24_Insurance renewal notice.pdf` |

## Keeping it tidy

- Empty 0_In often. Open items one by one: if it takes under two minutes, do it now; otherwise move it to the right folder or delete it.
- Once a week, do a "weekly review" of every folder. Clean up finished projects and pull out anything from 4_Someday-Maybe you want to start.
- Topic folders in 5_Reference are easiest to find in alphabetical order.

## When another style fits better

- Keeping material matters more than tracking actions → **PARA**
- You'd rather give everything a fixed number → **Johnny.Decimal**

Source: David Allen, *Getting Things Done* (Penguin, 2015), https://gettingthingsdone.com/
